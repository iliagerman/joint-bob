# Usage dashboard

Open **Costs** from the application header or with the configurable default shortcut **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+C**. The dashboard reports token usage and API-equivalent cost for the projects and conversations visible to the signed-in user. API-equivalent cost is an estimate of the corresponding metered API request; it does not allocate or amortize subscription plan fees.

The header shows when usage was last refreshed, a refresh button, and while a view loads, one line saying how many of its parts are ready and which ones it is still waiting for. Below it, the period sits beside chips for the filters in use; select a chip's × to drop that filter, or **+ Filter** for cluster, project, conversation, label, difficulty, model, engine and provider. When some sessions could not be read or some requests have no price, an amber **Estimated** badge says how many, and **Why?** explains that figures marked with an amber dot are lower bounds.

Existing conversation labels and recorded difficulty classifications can filter and group usage. Native costs reported by Pi are retained exactly. Where no native cost exists, Joint Bob uses the catalog snapshot captured with the request. Catalog prices describe the catalog available at capture time and are not an invoice.

Saved ledger totals appear immediately. The dashboard reads its parts separately (`GET /api/usage?sections=summary`, then the grouping on screen, the daily trend, and the other groupings), so totals show as soon as they are read and each grouping fills in when it arrives; a grouping that fails says so without blanking the rest. Transcript discovery then runs in the background. While it runs, the open dashboard polls only `GET /api/usage/status`, which is cheap, and re-reads usage when the refresh ends or, at most every 15 seconds, when saved usage has changed. Refresh requests are accepted without waiting for a scan. Coverage includes imported native transcripts and replicated ledger events. Historical backfill may be partial, copied fork records are excluded, and Kiro sessions whose token details are unavailable remain explicitly unknown rather than being estimated. Draft sessions are not treated as missing usage.

Subscription prices are grouped by the actual harness you select, not inferred from provider identity. Multiple account plans can belong to one harness. Older provider-only records remain under **Unassigned — choose a harness** until you edit them; they are never assigned automatically.

Conversation costs are paged 20 at a time and ordered by known cost, then stable conversation ID. Charts show only positive known API-equivalent cost: the eight largest projects, five largest models, and populated days from the last 30 days. Remaining known cost is preserved as Other. Missing and unpriced usage is labeled and is never drawn as zero.

## Compare periods

Four figures lead: month to date against the previous month over the same days, where the month ends at its pace so far (shown from the third day), the daily average against the three months before, and week to date against last week. **Month to date** and **Week to date** switch the chart and the analysis without reading anything again.

The chart shows each period's running total in its own colour, with the current period's pace dashed ahead of today. Hover anywhere on it for every period's total that day; select a period in the legend to hide its line. The table under it lists each period's total by the same day, its full total, and how the current period compares.

Beside the chart, **What changed** sets the period so far against the previous one over the same days: the change in cost, the projects, models and skills that moved most, and a table of totals (cost, requests, tokens, cost per request, cache read, failed tool calls, turns, median turn, failed turns, skill turns) with rises that are bad news marked. Selecting a project or model narrows the whole dashboard to it; selecting a skill opens it in Skills & time. Totals, skills and turn times are read separately and each appears as soon as both periods have arrived. It follows the filters, and turn figures are left out under a model, provider, label or difficulty filter.

## Skills & time

The **Skills & time** tab uses the same filters and answers two questions: which skills do agents load and what do those turns look like, and when and how long do turns run.

A skill counts as used when an agent loads it: a successful read of its `SKILL.md`, Claude's Skill tool, or a `/skill:name` command. Each skill is one row, whichever folder or node it was loaded from. A row shows its turns and conversations, a trend of turns per day, the cost of those turns in total and per turn, the share of tool calls that failed, the median turn time, and a status: **Fails often**, **Slow**, **Costly** or **OK**. A figure behind a status is highlighted. A turn that loads several skills is counted under each of them, so rows overlap and do not add up.

The totals above the list compare turns that loaded a skill with the rest of the period; a figure that cannot be read leaves the strip and a line under it says why. The cards under them say what stands out, each leading with its figure and what it is set against: the most used skill, and a skill whose turns fail, run or cost clearly more than turns with any skill (at least five turns; one and a half times the comparison for failures and cost, twice for time). These are observations; they do not show that a skill caused anything.

**Not used** lists the skills installed on this node that no agent loaded in the period and scope. Kiro sessions and skills that exist only on another node are not covered. **Days** joins cost and requests with turns, failed turns, and the median turn, queue and tool-call time for each day. **Hours** adds the period up by local hour. Both need a period of 151 days or less, and turn times cannot be filtered by model, provider, label or difficulty. **Clusters**, shown on a node that is in one, lists every cluster with its members, shared projects, requests and what those projects cost; select one, or choose it in the cluster report's own controls, for its projects, nodes, models and skills. **Repeated work** looks for repeated conversation titles in one project and drafts a skill or script for you to review; it reads saved titles, turn times and usage only, and installs nothing without approval.

Selecting a skill opens it in a panel beside the list, which keeps its place. **Statistics** starts with what stands out about the skill, each against all skill turns and with a likely cause where the data shows one (the model behind most failures or most cost, the weekday it is mostly used on, a better than average cache read). Then come its turns, cost, cost per turn, failed tool calls, median turn and cache read, turns per day, cost by model, its costliest conversations and its projects. **SKILL.md** shows the skill's `SKILL.md` as installed on this node (up to 200 KB), where it lives, its size and the other files beside it, and a Copy button. Skills nobody loaded open straight on SKILL.md. A skill that exists only on another node cannot be read here.

**Suggestions** is read when you first open it for the current filters and kept until you select **Check again** or change the filters. It is one list, the most money behind each suggestion first: **New skill** for a kind of work worth a skill (a pair of words in the titles of at least three conversations on at least two different days, with a match strength and a note when an installed skill may already cover it), **Fix** for a skill whose turns fail clearly more often than other skill turns, **Merge** for a skill described almost the same as another, and **Remove** for one never loaded in the usage this node has recorded or not loaded for 90 days. Filter by kind at the top, which also shows what the work behind the suggestions cost. Select a suggestion's title for its evidence, such as the conversations behind a new skill. Each has one action (Draft skill, See skill, Compare, Read it) and **Dismiss**, which hides it until the page reloads. Removal is judged over everything recorded, and ideas for new skills follow the filters. It uses local usage and conversation titles only: no model and no transcript text. It never installs or removes anything. Draft a skill from a suggestion, read the exact text, and approve it to add it to the project.

Lists fit the window and page instead of scrolling. Each report is read separately, so one that fails says so and leaves the others in place.

Skills Claude loads through its Skill tool are counted from this version on. Each Claude transcript is read once more in the background to find them, so earlier Claude use fills in after the first refresh.

Claude may report its login method and subscription type through its own local `auth status --json` command. Joint Bob keeps only those two bounded fields; account identities and raw command output are discarded. This reported plan is not a billed price. Pi, Kiro, and harnesses without a safe plan/price status interface are shown as unsupported. Detection never reads credential files, signs in, persists a plan, infers a fee, or reports quota.

Subscription prices and quota windows are manual records only. Joint Bob does not claim to query, refresh, or automate provider quotas. The timestamp shown for a quota is when that manual snapshot was changed; editing only a plan price does not refresh it. Subscription setup loads and saves independently of usage estimates, so it remains available if usage refresh is slow or unavailable.
