# Control center

The control center is one screen for everything that is running. It replaces the canvas: choose **Control center** (the target icon) in the toolbar above the project list, or press the same shortcut (Control+Option+V by default). It takes the whole window: the project and conversation side bars come back when you leave. **Exit**, or **Esc** twice (the first press asks, with a **Leave** button), takes you back to where you were. On a phone it is Bob's chat (see **On a phone**).

## The board

Every conversation that is running, one that waits for you, and one that finished and waits for review, in all the projects you can see. Each lane (**Needs you**, **Running**, **Done · ready for review**) groups its conversations by project, projects in name order and the newest conversation first, so one project's work sits together; the lane's count stands out, in blue for what waits for review. Each project's group is drawn in the project's color, with its name in that color, so projects tell apart at a glance; its chip in the project filter and its messages in Bob's chat have the same color. The color is the one chosen in the project's settings, as the project list shows it; a project without one gets a steady color from the same palette. When two projects look alike, choose another color for one of them in its settings.

- **Needs you** cards show a step waiting for your OK (a Guardian review: what the agent wants to run, with **Allow once** and **Block**), an agent whose auto respond stopped on something only you can settle, or one Bob checked and found waiting for you. Opening such a card puts you in control at once, with the message box ready, so you can answer without pressing **Take control**; closing it gives it back to Bob. Looking at it is not answering it: a card Bob found waiting for you stays here until you answer the conversation, close it, or press **Leave it** or **Push it on**.
- **Running** cards are marked **Running** on top, in green with a ring that turns, for as long as the agent works or its background tasks run. They show the title, the agent and model, and where the conversation stands. The first line, in the accent color, says what the turn is doing now: the tool it runs and what it runs on (*Bash · npm test*), or the last line it wrote, or *Thinking…*. Under it is the first sentence of the summary's *Current state*. Hover a card for its brief: it opens beside the card, in larger type, with the whole title, what the turn does now, and the summary laid out by section as in the conversation's Activity panel. It stays open while the pointer is on it, so a long one can be scrolled. While something runs, the board reads them again every 10 seconds. A conversation that runs on another of your machines keeps its summary and its turn there: the board asks that machine for them, once per machine each time it is drawn, so its card shows them wherever you open the board. A machine that does not answer within a moment, or runs an older version, leaves its cards without them and is asked again a minute later.
- **Done · ready for review** cards are marked **To review** with a blue edge, and have a blue **Review** button. It opens the conversation over the board to read what it did; once you have looked, it leaves this lane. Take control is in the opened conversation. A conversation marked done leaves the board until it runs again: its card shows a check with *Done* and goes, and the cards around it glide into its place. A card that leaves for any other reason fades out. A card Bob checked says **✓ Bob checked: done** (see **Is it really done?** below).
- **Who drives it** is on the card: **⚙ System drives** when auto respond answers the agent for you, **👁 Bob watches** for a conversation Bob started (he tells you when it needs you), **✋ You're in it** once you take control. A card with none of these is yours alone. The line under the title counts them for the board (*2 driven by the system · 1 watched by Bob*).
- **✓ Done** is at the top right of every card, beside its color: it closes the conversation after the same question about what to learn as marking it done in its project. On a card whose agent is working it stops the conversation first (its turn, auto respond, and any step waiting for an answer), on whichever of your machines it runs, and its hover says so. Bob's check of it ends, and his items about it are cleared.
- A project's own buttons, such as its **Dashboard**, are on the project's group, not on each card.
- **Every card is the same size.** The title gets two lines and the status two; longer text is cut, and you can hover it to read all of it. On a **Needs you** card, the step waiting for you replaces the status, with **Allow once** and **Block** (*· 2 more* when several wait, and the next one shows once you answer).
- Click a card to open its conversation over the board, to **watch**: you read it live while Bob keeps driving, and there is no message box. **Take control** (in the open conversation, or on the card) brings the message box back, inside the window, and tells Bob and auto respond to stay out until you give it back (a banner in the conversation says so, and has the button). **Close** or **Esc** goes back to the board, and closing hands the conversation back to Bob. Leaving the control center closes it too.

The board refreshes from the signals that already keep the running list current, and from the server when Bob has something new. The control center's icon shows a number when something waits for you (a step to approve, or a message from Bob with buttons still on it), even when the control center is closed.

## Tabs

The board is the first tab, **Live agents**. **Project overview** shows one project at a glance: its conversations as short headlines (running, ready for review, started by Bob), what changed recently (branch, uncommitted files, last commits), and what its project knowledge holds, with **Ask Bob about this project** and **New task here** pointing Bob's chat at it. More tabs are added by the features below.

## Making it yours

- **Project filter.** The chips above the board show only the projects that have cards. Select one or several; **All** clears the filter.
- **Color.** The circle in a card's corner is a color picker. The whole card takes the color.
- **Reset** clears the colors and the filter.
- **Bob's chat** sits on the left of the board. **⇄** in its header moves it to the right and back; drag its edge to make it wider.

Colors, the filter, and the chat's side and width are kept with your account. The chat's side as kept before 2.235.0 is not used: the chat starts on the left once, and **⇄** moves it back. Bob's answers are formatted (lists, bold, code), not shown as raw Markdown.

## Bob

Bob is the chat beside the board. It acts for you across projects. Drag its edge to make it wider, and use the arrows button to put it on the left or the right. When you send a message, Bob acknowledges it at once (*Got it. Working on “…”*), with how long he has worked on it so far, until his answer comes.

Each message goes to the decision model first (TypeSafe or Laya, your choice in Bob's settings, with a key from the first project that has one attached). In one request it judges what the message is (a question, a task, an automation, a change to how the app looks or something else), how big the job is, which project it is about, and whether auto respond suits the work; with many projects they are asked in groups at the same time. The line under Bob's replies shows what was decided and how sure the model was.

- **A task in a project, and the model is at least as sure as your threshold (90% unless you change it):** Bob starts it as a normal conversation in that project, with Bob's agent unless you said otherwise. It is marked **started by Bob** on its card and appears in the project's conversation list. When the work is routine, and your setting **Auto respond** allows it, Bob turns on auto respond for it with stop instructions that always include plans to approve, decisions that change what users see, and production or destructive steps.
- **A task that belongs to a conversation the project already has** goes there instead of starting a new one, when the decision model is sure which ("also adjust the width" after a menu task). A conversation that runs on another machine, or that you have taken control of, is left alone: Bob says so and starts a new one.
- **Name an agent** in the message ("do it with Pi", "using Claude Code") and the work runs on that agent instead of Bob's own. If this machine cannot run it, Bob says so and starts nothing.
- **Anything else, a threshold not met, or no decision model:** Bob's own agent answers. It sees what is running and what waits, with where each conversation runs, whether its card has a summary and, for one this machine runs, its transcript, plus each project's recent work. It can read and search files but change nothing. When one of your projects is Joint Bob's own source (its package is `joint-bob` and it has `docs/`), Bob is told where it is, and runs there when no project is chosen. So when you ask why Joint Bob shows or does something, he reads the transcript, the docs and, if needed, the code, and says what he checked and what the cause is.
- **Project.** Choose a project in the chat to skip the guess.
- **Files and images.** Attach files with **+**, paste a screenshot into the message box, or drop files on it. The message box's limits apply: 4 images and 6 files, each under 4 MB. Write what Bob should do with them too. They go where the message goes: they are saved in the project, and Bob's agent is told where they are, with read-only access to open them and the images themselves. They also go to the conversation Bob starts or continues, as if you had attached them there. Your message in Bob's chat lists them (*Attached: screen.png*). Requests that do not reach an agent leave them out: creating or moving a project, scheduling, moving jobs and making dashboards.

Bob keeps its conversation: every message and reply is saved with the project it concerns and how much it matters (**Needs OK**, **Urgent**, **Needs you**, **Report**, **Done**, **FYI**).

A message about one conversation is a card in its project's color, so you can tell projects apart at a glance:
- **On top:** the project, and under it the conversation's name. Click the name to open that conversation; the card does not repeat it.
- **Under that:** how much it matters, who raised it in plain words (*Bob checked it when it stopped*, *Guardian holds a risky step for your OK*, *Auto respond stopped*, a webhook…), and when. While the message waits, this line also shows where its conversation stands now, as on the board: **Running now**, **Waits for you** or **Done · ready for review**. It follows the board, which is read again whenever anything changes and every 10 seconds while an agent runs, so a conversation that started again (after an update, say) shows it at once.
- **Then** what it says and its buttons.

Bob's answers to you, reports and your own messages read as chat, with who wrote them, the project, how much they matter and when on top. How it was decided (*Decision model 96%*) is the small line under the message. Messages that need you carry buttons; a button runs once.

**Reply, Done and Dismiss on every message.** Besides its own buttons, every message of Bob's in the chat has three quieter ones (a Guardian step has only its own: **Allow once** and **Block**):
- **Reply** opens a box under the message. A reply to a message about a conversation goes into that conversation as you, as if you had typed it there (a busy one gets it after its current turn), and the message is cleared. The machine that runs the conversation sends it, so this works from any of your machines once both are on this version. A reply to anything else goes to Bob, saying which message it answers, and that message is marked answered. Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line, Esc closes the box. What you type stays while new messages arrive.
- **Done** (on a message about a conversation that has no **Done** of its own) closes that conversation as marking it done in its project does, and clears the message; the card leaves the board with it.
- **Dismiss** only clears the message. It stays under **✓ Cleared**.

**Items leave once they are settled.** A message with buttons is an item that waits for you. Press one of its buttons and it is settled: it glows, a check draws itself with the button you pressed (*Done*, *Abandon*, *Push it on*…), and it slides out of the chat while the rest closes up. Bob also keeps the list current by himself:
- **What the facts settle is cleared at once**, when you review or close a conversation (in its project, from the board or in the chat), a conversation you have an item about starts or ends a turn, Bob checks a conversation that stopped, or a Guardian step is answered, and otherwise every 2 minutes. An item that asks you about a conversation goes when the conversation was marked done, runs again or moved on since, or a newer item about the same conversation came; looking at it does not answer it. Bob's note that he told a conversation to go on goes as soon as the conversation runs again, since the board shows who drives it; when that turn already ended, it goes once he checked it (what it came to is his next message, or the board), or you looked at the conversation. A Guardian item goes when its step was answered or timed out. The check reads each conversation as your project's list shows it, wherever it runs.
- **The decision model judges the rest**: items with no conversation (a question about a project, an automation, moving jobs, a memory change, a webhook) and items whose conversation the facts do not settle. Once an item is 10 minutes old, the decision model reads it with what happened since: your later messages to Bob, his answers, and the items cleared after it, with why. It clears the item only when it is sure that this log settles it, at your confidence threshold or above. Unsure, or no decision model, keeps the item. The same evidence is judged once; the item is asked about again only when something in it changed, at most 8 items per check, and only while Bob is not paused. It is asked by the machine that wrote the item.
- **Notices that lost their buttons are cleared.** When an item was pressed or cleared on one of your machines while another ran a version before 2.231.0, that one took its buttons off without keeping that it was settled, so it stayed in the chat with nothing to press. Each check clears such notices from Bob's review checks, Guardian and auto respond.
- **Cleared** (next to the importance filters) lists the cleared items with when and why (*You chose “Done”.*, *You looked at it.*, *Bob's decision model found it settled by what happened since (92%).*), so nothing disappears without a trace. Reports are never cleared: their buttons are shortcuts.

**Focus on one project.** Choosing a project in the chat's filter focuses the whole control center on it: the chat shows that project's messages, the board shows only its cards, and a new message goes to it. Choosing one project in the board's chips does the same, and **All projects** or **All** shows everything again. Filter by importance with **Needs me**, **Reports** and **FYI**.

**Settings** (in Bob's header) choose Bob's own agent (harness, model and effort), the decision model, the minimum confidence, whether Bob's responder answers for the agents Bob starts (**Bob decides per task**, **always**, or **never**, so they wait for you), and whether Bob asks before saving anything to its memory. The responder is auto respond with Bob's own agent and stop instructions. They are kept with your account.

**Monitoring.** The button in Bob's header pauses everything Bob does on its own: telling you about things, and auto respond that Bob turned on. Agents keep running; questions wait on the board. **Restore** (in the banner) turns it back on. Auto respond you turned on yourself keeps running.

**Production and destructive steps always ask you.** Guardian's rule "Production or destructive steps" checks every command with the decision model and asks you before it runs. Bob's settings show whether it is on; an administrator can turn it on there (it also turns Guardian on for the machine). When a step waits, it shows on the board, in Bob's chat (with **Allow once** and **Block**) and in the conversation. Bob never answers these for you; pressing the button is you answering. When no one answers within 10 minutes, the step is blocked.

When auto respond ends (the agent needs you, the limit was reached, or a turn failed), Bob tells you in the chat with the reason and buttons to open the conversation, turn auto respond on again, or take it over.

## How Joint Bob looks

Ask Bob to change how the app looks: "switch to the midnight theme", "make the conversation text bigger", "make everything a bit smaller". Bob hands the request to Joint Bob's display agent, which decides the change and reports back.

- **What it changes** is Settings → Appearance (the logo opens it): the theme, the size of the whole app, and the size of each part (Projects panel, Conversations panel, Conversation, Activity pane, Headers). As when you change them there, they are kept in the browser you asked from, and your other devices keep their own. For anything else, such as fonts, colors or layout, it says it cannot.
- **It is applied at once.** Bob's reply says what changed, and **Undo** puts the earlier settings back.
- **It runs on a lighter model.** The display agent is Bob's agent on a model from your routing profile, or Joint Bob's default one when you chose none. The decision model (Jev) picks among the profile's lighter half of levels, so it never gets one of the strongest models. Without a decision model key, the lightest level runs. The line under the reply says which (*Display agent · haiku · TypeSafe (Jev) chose level 1*).
- **Only from Bob's chat in the app.** From Telegram there are no display settings to change, so Bob says to ask in the app.

## Bob's memory

Bob keeps its own notes as **topic files**: one markdown file per subject, with a name, a one-line description and a text, linked to other topics with `[[topic-name]]`. They are about you and how you work (corrections, preferences, decisions), not about a project's code: a project's own rules stay in its `.agents/knowledge/`. Each account has its own folder, `control-center/memory/<account>/` in the node's data directory, with an `INDEX.md` (one line per topic, rebuilt after every write) next to the topic files. Memory is copied to your other machines (your twins): each topic is sent when it changes, and the newest write of a topic wins. The history of changes stays on the machine where the change was made.

**Using it.** Before Bob's agent answers, Bob always has the list of topics with one line each. In one batched request the decision model is asked, for each topic (up to 30, as many as its limit allows, best word matches first), whether it matters for your message; the text of the topics it says yes to is added, and the line under Bob's reply says `Read memory: release-gate`. A topic the model is less sure about than your threshold, or every topic when no decision model is set up, goes to a small agent step with no tools that picks from the list. If that fails, Bob still gets the list.

**Learning.** After Bob answers, the decision model judges whether the exchange taught something worth remembering (a correction, a preference, a decision). Only a yes (or, below your threshold or with no decision model, the agent's own call) lets an agent step write or update one topic. It never saves secrets, and it cannot rewrite a topic it was not shown. Nothing happens while Bob's monitoring is paused.

**Every write is a change** you can read: the topic, the text before and after, why Bob saved it, the message that taught it, and when. Your own edits are changes too. With **Ask me before Bob saves anything** on, a change is stored as pending and the topic is not touched until you keep it; Bob posts a message in its chat with **Keep** and **Revert**. Without it Bob's write is made at once and shows as *To review*, with a **Revert** button in the chat.

**The Bob's memory tab** has two parts, both paged to fit the height:

- **Topics.** The list with one line each and a mark on the topics Bob read for your last message. Open a topic to read it, **Edit** it, or **Delete** it (after a confirmation). Links between topics are clickable, a link to a topic that does not exist opens a new one with that name, and each topic shows what links to it and its recent history. **+ Topic** creates one. The search looks at names, descriptions and texts. A topic can be tagged with a project; it then follows the project filter of the board, and topics about no project always show.
- **What Bob changed.** Each change as a before and after, with the reason and what taught Bob. **Keep** confirms it (and writes it, when it was pending), **Edit** lets you change the text first (your version is kept and the change shows it was edited), **Revert** puts the earlier text back, or removes a topic the change created. Reverting a deletion brings the topic back. Nothing is removed from the history, and nothing is deleted without you.
- **Ask me before Bob saves anything** is Bob's own setting, also in Bob's settings.

## Automations

The **Automations** tab lists every scheduled task and webhook in the projects you can see, and the reports Bob writes for you, in one table: an on/off switch, the name, the type (*schedule*, *webhook* or *Bob*), the project, when it runs or what a webhook does with a call, how the last run went, and **Edit** and **Delete**. It follows the project filter of the board, shows its own chips for it, shows the number of rows on the tab, and pages to the height it has instead of scrolling. A machine in your cluster that does not answer is named above the table; the rest still shows.

It is not a second copy. A scheduled task or webhook made, switched, edited or deleted here is the same one the project's **Scheduled tasks** screen and **Settings → Webhooks** show, because the table calls their own commands (the owning machine runs them, as there). Only the person who made a task or webhook can switch or edit it; anyone who sees its project can delete it, as before.

- **+ Scheduled task** asks for the project, a name, what the agent should do, and when, like a meeting in a calendar: **Once** on a date, or every hour, day, week or month, at a time, from a first day (optional, except for a monthly task, which repeats on that date) and **Until** an optional last day. It runs on this machine with its usual agent. **+ Webhook** asks for the project, a name and what the agent should do with each call; the secret address is shown once, when you save it. **Edit** opens the same small form with what is stored, and **More options…** opens the full editor (agent and model, auto reply, other machine, quiet hours, senders, files) for anything the small form leaves out. A schedule the small form cannot show (every few minutes, quiet hours, chosen days) is edited there too.
- **Delete** asks again on the same button before it acts.
- **Bob's reports** are owned by Bob (type *Bob*, project *All projects* or the ones you chose). **+ Bob report** adds a morning report (08:00) or an evening report (19:00) at the time you set; you can have several. **Send now** writes one at once, also while Bob is paused, because you asked.

**Bob writes the reports.** At its time Bob collects, for the projects the report covers, what finished and waits for your review (the morning report looks back 14 hours, the evening report 13), what is still running, scheduled tasks and webhook calls that failed, how many scheduled runs went fine, and what each project cost in that time, and Bob's own agent (the harness, model and effort in Bob's settings) writes it from those facts and nothing else. It is posted in Bob's chat as a **Report**, labelled with the project when it covers one and *General* otherwise. For each of up to three things that wait for you there is a button that reads its summary, and for each of up to three failed scheduled tasks a button that runs it again; a button works once, and the answer brings the other buttons along. If the agent cannot write it, the facts are posted as they are and the line under the report says why.

One timer serves all of Bob's reports: it waits for the earliest report that is due, writes it, and waits for the next; saving, switching or deleting a report points it at the new earliest time. Nothing polls. While **Bob is paused** a report that comes due is skipped, marked *skipped* in the table, and not kept for later. A report that comes due while the server is off is also skipped (up to 30 minutes late is still written). Bob's reports live on the machine where you made them; they are not copied to your other machines.

**From Bob's chat.** Say what you want: "what scheduled tasks do I have?", "pause the nightly release check", "move the nightly scheduled task to 03:30", "add a scheduled task next Tuesday at 14:30 to check the launch", "add a scheduled task every month on the 15th until March to send the invoices", "also send me an evening report at 7pm", "delete the weekly dependency audit". Bob is told today's date and weekday where you are, so "next Tuesday" is the Tuesday on your calendar. The decision model has already labelled the message *automation* (or you named scheduled tasks, webhooks, schedules or reports and it was not a task for a project); one step of Bob's agent then turns it into a small, checked intent (list, pause, resume, change, delete or create, with fields), and it can only name automations and projects from the lists it was given. Nothing you wrote is ever run as a command.

- **Listing, pausing and resuming** happen at once, because you asked for exactly that, and use the same functions as the table, so the chat and the table agree.
- **Changing, deleting and creating** first post a message labelled *Needs you* that says exactly what will happen, with a button (**Change it**, **Delete** or **Create**, and **Cancel**). Nothing changes until you press it; a button works once and comes back if the action fails. A change renames or re-times one automation and keeps everything else: a scheduled task takes a new name, frequency, time or weekday (a schedule with options the small form cannot show is changed under **More options**), a Bob report a new name or time, a webhook a new name.
- **A webhook** is not created from the chat, because its secret address is shown only once; Bob says so and points you to **+ Webhook**.

## Is it really done?

A conversation that stops and waits for review is not always finished: the agent may have listed next steps, asked whether to go on, or stopped after an error it could fix. So when a turn ends, Bob checks.

- **When.** About 20 seconds after the turn ends, on the machine that runs the conversation, if nothing else moves it: no message waits in its queue, auto respond is off (auto respond already answers for the agent, and when it stops on that turn it has already brought the conversation to you), and you have not taken control. A scheduled task's conversation is left to its own settings.
- **Whose.** The person whose conversation it is: who sent the turn, or, for a conversation Bob started or pushed on, the person he did it for. Nobody else's Bob acts on it, and nothing happens while Bob is paused. It works on each of your machines: using Bob on one is enough, and your Bob settings follow you.
- **How it is decided.** The decision model reads the request, the conversation's summary and the end of the agent's last answer, and judges one of three: **done**, **not done** (work the agent can carry on with by itself), or **waits for you** (a decision, a plan to approve, credentials or access, or a go-ahead for a production or destructive step). When the model is missing, cannot answer, or is less sure than your threshold, Bob's own agent reads the same and decides. The line under Bob's message and the card's hover say which.
- **What Bob does.**
  - *Done*: nothing; the card stays in review, marked **✓ Bob checked: done**.
  - *Not done*: Bob tells the agent to go on ("Continue with the task: you stopped before it was done… If you need something only I can decide, ask me"), says so in his chat, and the card shows **↻ Bob told it to go on (1 of 3)** and **⚙ System drives**. After three pushes in a row with no message from you between them, Bob stops and brings it to you instead.
  - *Waits for you*: the card moves to **Needs you** with what it waits for, and stays there when you open it to look, until you answer it, close it, or press **Leave it** or **Push it on**. Bob's chat has it with:
    - **Push it on**: tell it to go on anyway.
    - **Done**: close it, as marking it done in its project does.
    - **Abandon**: stop it (its turn, auto respond, a step waiting for an answer) and close it without finishing.
    - **Open**: read it first.
  - When Bob told it to go on, his message has **Abandon** too, in case you would rather drop it. Done and Abandon end Bob's check of that conversation, and it leaves the board and the chat: the card and the message each go with a check that says what you did, and Bob's other items about that conversation are cleared too.
- **Your setting.** In Bob's settings, **When work stops unfinished**: **Bob tells it to go on** (the default), **Bob asks me first** (a *Needs you* message with **Push it on** and **Leave it**), or **Leave it in review**.

## Workspaces, projects and conversations

Bob can create and arrange your work: "create a project called Billing in Clients", "create a workspace called Clients", "rename the slice 10 conversation to Admin overview", "move Billing to Personal", "mark the errors conversation as done", "delete the old spike conversation", "start a conversation in DAK to check the payout job", "ask the slice 10 conversation how to review it", "write the summary of the slice 10 conversation", "delete the Clients workspace". The decision model labels such a message *organize*, and a message that asks Bob to ask, tell or send something to a conversation always goes this way. One step of Bob's agent then turns it into a small, checked intent. That intent can only name your own workspaces and projects, and your conversations: those on the board, plus each project's eight most recent ones, so an idle conversation you name is found too.

- **Every change waits for your button.** Bob says exactly what will happen (*Create the project "Billing" in the workspace "Clients"?*) with **Create it**, **Rename it**, **Move it**, **Mark done**, **Start it**, **Send it**, **Delete it** or **Remove it**, and **Cancel**. Removing or deleting is a warning button.
- **Asking a conversation.** **Send it** sends your message to that conversation as you, as if you had typed it there; a busy one answers after its current turn. When it is done, its answer comes back to Bob's chat as a report with **Open**. Only a conversation this machine runs can be asked from here; Bob says so for one on another machine.
- **Deleting a conversation** removes it and its whole history for good, wherever it runs, the same as deleting it from the project's list.
- **Summaries are written at once.** "Write the summary of the slice 10 conversation", or "update the summaries of these three", needs no button: a summary changes nothing else. Bob says which ones he is writing, and posts each summary in the chat as a report with **Open** when it is written, or says why it was not (no answer yet, or the machine that runs it cannot be reached). It is the same summary the conversation's Activity panel and the board's cards show, written by the same writer (Settings → Conversations), on the machine that runs the conversation: for a conversation on another of your machines, Bob asks that machine to write it, so every board shows it. That machine needs this version or later. Up to 10 at a time. Asking *why* a card has no summary gets an answer, not a summary; Bob says you can ask him to write it.
- **It is the same as doing it yourself.** The change runs as you, through the functions behind the Projects screen and Settings → Workspaces, with the same checks: a new project gets its folder in your projects home; moving a project moves its folder; removing a project takes it out of Joint Bob and leaves its folder on disk; a workspace is deleted only when it is empty, and Bob says what is still in it.
- **A new conversation** starts like a task Bob starts: with Bob's agent, marked started by Bob, with auto respond when your setting says always.
- Bob refuses what it cannot find, or a workspace name you already have, without asking.

## Dashboards

The **Dashboards** tab shows the dashboards you can see, as cards, in two groups: **Projects** (a project's own dashboard) and **For me** (a dashboard of your workspace, not tied to one project). It is the existing dashboards feature (the dashboard-creator agent, the bundled widgets, script or AI refresh, approval of what other machines share); nothing is copied. Conversation dashboards stay in their conversations.

- **A card** names the project (or the workspace), the title, who made it (*made by Bob*, *yours*, *shared with you*), when it was refreshed and whether it refreshes by **script** (no AI) or by **AI** (*costs AI*). It follows the board's project filter (a project's dashboards, and a personal one whose workspace holds a chosen project), puts the number on the tab, and pages to the height it has instead of scrolling.
- **One click** shows the dashboard over the board, in the existing sandboxed frame. **Refresh** runs its script, or, for an AI dashboard, the button says **Refresh with AI** and a note says each refresh uses AI. **Ask Bob to change it** sends your words to Bob's chat and the dashboard agent edits the dashboard. **Full view** opens the dashboards screen for editing, sharing and approving. A dashboard another machine shared is not run until you read and approve it there.
- **A Dashboard button on each project's group** on the board opens that project's dashboard. A project without one shows **+ Dashboard**, which writes the start of the request in Bob's message box with the project chosen, for you to finish.
- **Unfinished work shows too.** A dashboard Bob is still building shows *Bob is building it…*; one the agent could not finish stays as a draft, and a click opens the full view to carry on.

**Ask Bob from the chat.** "Create a dashboard for Julian showing bugs per day", "make me a dashboard of what needs my attention", or both in one message. After the decision model has labelled the message, a small check asks it two yes/no questions in one request (a dashboard for a project? one for you?); below Bob's confidence, or with no decision model, Bob's own agent answers the same questions as a small JSON object that is checked before it is used, and only for a message that mentions a dashboard. Nothing else is read from free text.

- Bob answers at once that it is building, and starts the dashboard-creator agent for each dashboard in the background. When one is done Bob saves it, makes it the one that project (or workspace) shows (older ones stay as alternatives), refreshes a script dashboard once so it has data, and says so in the chat as a **Done** message with an **Open dashboards** button that switches to the tab. An agent that fails is reported as **Needs you**, with the draft kept.
- A dashboard that refreshes by **AI** costs money, so Bob does not run its first refresh: the message says so and you press **Refresh with AI** when you want data.
- **Which project, which workspace.** The project comes from the message or from the chat's Project box. A personal dashboard goes to the workspace of that project, or to the only workspace that has projects. If it is not clear, Bob asks and builds nothing.
- Bob records the dashboards it made, per person, to label them. Saving a dashboard Bob built is the same save as in the creator view, so it is yours to edit, share or delete like any other. Bob does not change a dashboard unless you ask with **Ask Bob to change it**, and a refresh never runs on its own.
- Dashboards cannot run agents or scripts on a multi-user node yet; Bob says so instead of starting.

## Moving jobs to another machine

A conversation runs on the machine that owns it. Moving one is the ordinary ownership transfer ("Runs on"), started from the board or from Bob; nothing is copied by hand and the transfer's own rules stay (the transcript and the project's files must be on the destination, and a ticket's conversation moves with its ticket, so it is listed as *can't move*).

- **Select** (in the board's toolbar) puts a box on every card; click a card to pick it. The bar at the bottom has **Move to** (the machines you can reach, an offline one greyed), **Stop and move now** and **Move after current turn**, and a line saying which of the picked conversations cannot move to that machine, and why, before you press anything. Under the cards, each machine with unfinished agents shows *Mac · 4 running* and **Move all…**, which turns Select on with every unfinished agent on that machine picked.
- **Move after current turn** waits for each running turn to end, then moves the conversation. One that is waiting for you (not in a turn) moves at once. **Stop and move now** asks first, then for each conversation stops the running turn, moves it and sends it one message: *"Continue where you stopped: you were moved from <machine> to <machine>. Check what was half done before repeating anything."* A conversation that was not in a turn is moved without that message. The original request is never sent again, and the continue message is queued once and never retried: if its delivery is uncertain the result says so and you open the conversation.
- **Following a batch.** A panel under the bar lists each conversation (waiting, stopping, moving, moved, can't move, failed) with its reason; **Stop waiting** leaves the ones still waiting where they are. A batch that waits keeps waiting for up to six hours per conversation; it lives in memory on the machine you are signed in to, so restarting that machine ends the waiting (nothing half-moved is left, and nothing is replayed).
- **What cannot move, and why.** Before anything moves, the destination is asked, as itself, whether the conversation could run there: the project must be set up on it, it must accept runs from this machine, the conversation's harness must be installed and signed in, and the model must be offered. A machine on an older version cannot answer and is listed as such. A conversation already on the destination is left alone, and one that another batch is moving is refused.
- **Story and Overview.** Every card has **Story** (the change story) and **Overview** (the two-host Activity overview). Both read the conversation that is open, its project, machine and ticket, so a card opens its conversation first (as clicking the card does) and then presses the same Story or Overview button the toolbar has; the generators and viewers are the existing ones.

**Ask Bob.** "Move all my running jobs from the Mac to the homeserver" (or "move the billing job to the homeserver"). Before the usual decision, a small check asks the decision model, in one request, whether the message asks to move jobs and which machines and conversation it names; below Bob's confidence, or with no decision model, Bob's agent answers the same questions as JSON that is checked before use (the agent reads only a message that has a word for moving in it or names one of your machines). The check only runs when your cluster has more than one machine. Bob answers with a message labelled *Needs you*: what moves and when, what cannot move and why, and three buttons, **Move N**, **Stop and move all now** and **Cancel**. That one answer covers the whole batch; the buttons disappear once pressed. Bob checks everything again when you press, starts the batch, and reports the result in the chat when it is over (*Done*, or *Needs you* when something did not move). With two machines, "move everything off the Mac" means the other one; with more, Bob asks which.

## Incoming triggers

Each call a webhook accepts is also an **incoming trigger** for everyone who uses Bob (has opened the control center) and can see the webhook's project. A trigger is kept in the inbox (the newest 200 per person) with the webhook, the project, a title, a one-line summary and a short excerpt of the call. The webhook itself does exactly what it was set up to do; Bob adds nothing to it. A webhook behind a connected Telegram bot is a chat with an assistant, not a trigger, and is skipped.

- **Title and line.** They come from the payload and are cut to size (120 and 200 characters; a 4,000-character excerpt is kept). GitHub (`X-GitHub-Event`: issues, pull requests, workflow runs, pushes), GitLab, Alertmanager, CloudWatch alarms (also through SNS), Stripe and a few common fields (`title`, `subject`, `name`, `event`, `type`, `message`, `state`/`status`/`action`) are recognised; anything else shows the webhook's name and the first lines of the payload. Control and direction characters are removed. The payload is outside text: it is only ever shown as data and never followed.
- **Urgent or not.** The decision model rates each trigger with one Choice question (urgent: something broken in production, a security issue, someone blocked right now; not urgent: new issues and pull requests, test events, routine notifications), with the trigger text as state. Below your saved confidence threshold, or with no decision model, Bob's own agent rates it (a short turn with no tools that answers `{"urgency": "urgent" | "not-urgent", "reason": …}`, checked before use). If that cannot run either, the trigger is treated as not urgent and the list says why. Nothing in the payload is ever carried out. At most three are rated at once; a flood of calls waits its turn.
- **Already in hand, or known.** Every webhook starts its own conversation for each call. An urgent call waits for that run to start (up to a minute). Once it started, the call is in hand: it goes to the bell, labelled *urgent, but its webhook started a conversation on it*, not to the chat. Otherwise it is compared, in one request to the decision model, with what you already have in its project: the alerts still open in the chat, the conversations that run or were active in the last day and are not closed, and the alerts you had earlier today. Every answer has to be at your confidence threshold; otherwise Bob's agent answers the same questions as JSON, checked before use, and when nothing can tell, the call is posted.
  - **The same problem as an open alert** adds to that alert instead of a new one: it says *Happened 3 times so far.*, and its buttons act on the newest call.
  - **A conversation already works on it**: the call goes to the bell (*urgent, but “Fix the follow-up check” is working on it*), and an open alert about the same problem leaves the chat with that line.
  - **The same problem as an alert you had earlier today**: the bell (*urgent, but the same problem as an alert you had earlier today*).
  - **Otherwise** it is a new alert. When its webhook's run could not start, the alert says so and why, since nobody is on it.
- **Followed to their end.** A webhook's run often only opens a worktree and starts the conversation that does the work; that start is recorded, so Bob follows each call to the conversation working on it (for calls from before that, the conversation its run's last answer names, or the run's own). When that conversation stops, the decision model reads the call and its last answer and judges one of three; below your threshold Bob's agent judges, and one answer is judged once:
  - **Settled** (it fixed it, found an existing fix, or found nothing wrong): the call leaves the bell, and its alert the chat, with why (*Settled by “Fix CRM polling”: Existing fix found: PR #415, merged.*).
  - **In progress** (it handed the work on, investigates, or waits for CI or its own merge): the call waits in the bell, labelled *in hand: “…”* with what it does; older calls about the same conversation leave.
  - **Needs you** (credits, access, an approval, a decision, or it could not do it): one **Needs you** item in Bob's chat about that conversation, with **Open**, **Reply** and **Done**, unless one is open already, and the call leaves the bell.
  It is checked when a turn ends in a project with calls to follow, and once when the server starts, which also matches calls stored before their run was kept to the run their webhook accepted within seconds of them. Nothing is followed while Bob is paused.
- **Alerts leave the chat** on their own, checked every 5 minutes. One nobody acted on for 6 hours after its latest call moves to the bell (seen, so it does not add to the bell's count). One whose webhook's run started after it was posted leaves too, and so does one that a conversation in its project took up; the decision model is asked that only when the project's conversations changed, at most 8 alerts per person each time, and only while Bob is not paused. Each leaves with why under **✓ Cleared**.
- **Urgent ones** are a message in Bob's chat (importance *Urgent*, line `Webhook · <name> · decision model 95%`, or `… · decision model unsure, Bob checked`) with three buttons. **Investigate (read-only)** starts a conversation in that project the way Bob starts any task (marked *started by Bob*, using Bob's harness) with a brief that says the work is read-only, to propose rather than change anything, and quotes the trigger as data; it is the only thing Bob does for a trigger, and only when you press it. **Open** opens the project's Webhooks screen and leaves the other buttons in place. **Snooze 1h** posts the same message again in an hour (one timer, no polling; it survives a restart, and if Bob is paused then, the trigger waits in the bell instead).
- **The bell** is in Bob's header, with the number of new triggers that can wait. Its panel lists them a page at a time (title, project, when, and how it was rated: `not urgent (93%)`, `decision model unsure, Bob checked`, or the reason it could not be rated) with **Open** (the webhook's screen), **Ask Bob to triage** and **Dismiss**, and **Dismiss all** at the top. Opening the panel does not count them as seen: what it showed is marked seen when it closes. The panel follows new arrivals while it is open, and **Esc** closes it first. **Ask Bob to triage** sends a question through Bob's chat as if you had typed it (with that project chosen), asking whether it is urgent and what to do, with the trigger quoted as data; what you were typing stays.
- **Paused.** While Bob's monitoring is paused nothing is rated or posted. The call is still stored as new and shows in the bell as *not rated yet*; it is not rated after you restore Bob, and you can ask Bob to triage it.

The routes are `GET /api/control-center/triggers` (`filter=bell|urgent|all`, `page`, `size`; returns `triggers`, `total` and `newCount`), `GET /api/control-center/triggers/:id/triage`, `POST /api/control-center/triggers/seen` and `POST /api/control-center/triggers/dismiss` (`{ ids }` or `{ all: true }`, which leaves the urgent ones). The webhook code announces each accepted call through one line in `acceptWebhookCall` (`src/control-center/triggers-observe.ts`); everything else lives in `src/control-center/triggers-*.ts` and `src/server/control-center-triggers.ts`.

## On a phone

Below 1024 px wide the control center is Bob's chat, full screen: the board and its tabs are not shown as columns. The same chat, filters, settings and monitor switch as on a desktop, laid out for a thumb.

- **Only what needs you.** **Needs me** in the chat's filters shows only what needs you: steps waiting for your OK, urgent items and questions. While agents run, the board refreshes every few seconds, but the chips, the agents sheet and the project lists are drawn again only when what they show changed, so a tap is never lost and an open list stays open.

- **Getting there.** On a small phone (up to 700 px) the round Joint Bob handle's menu has a **Control center** tile in every view; between 700 and 1023 px it is in the app menu (the three lines at the top right). Both show the number of things waiting for you. The app icon has a **Control center** shortcut (long-press the icon; it opens `/?action=control-center`). **‹** at the top left, or the browser's back button, returns to where you were. If you narrow a window while the control center is open, it stays open and becomes the chat.
- **Header.** Bob's monitoring state (the pill pauses and restores everything Bob does on its own), the bell for incoming items, and Settings. Approvals and questions are cards in the chat, with their buttons: a red **Needs OK** card with **Allow once** and **Block** for a Guardian review, a yellow card for a question for you.
- **The strip of chips** counts what needs you: **n needs your OK** (Guardian reviews waiting for an answer), **n need you** (an agent that stopped for you, or whose auto respond stopped), **n running**, **n to review**. A chip appears only while it has something to show. **Dashboards** opens the dashboards tab over the chat, with **‹ Bob** to come back. Counts come from the board's own data (the same request the desktop makes), and the Control center entries use the same waiting count as the toolbar badge.
- **The agents sheet.** A chip opens a compact sheet over the lower part of the chat (tap the chip again, the **×**, or Esc to close it; the Joint Bob handle steps aside while it is open). Rows are grouped *Needs your OK*, *Needs you*, *Running*, *To review*, one agent per row with its project and what it is doing, and its action: **Allow once** and **Block** (the same Guardian decision the board's buttons make), **Answer** or **Review** (open the conversation), **Watch** (open it without taking control) and **Take control** or **Give back** (the board card's own action, so Bob stays out of the conversation until you give it back). The sheet pages to the height it has instead of scrolling; a page that starts in the middle of a group repeats its heading.
- **Composer.** Above the message box a project chip chooses which project the message is about (**Auto** lets Bob decide) and an agent chip shows Bob's own agent and opens Bob's settings. Enter or the round button sends. There is no voice button: the web app has no dictation yet.

Nothing here changes the desktop: the extra parts are hidden at 1024 px and wider.

## Assistants

The **Assistants** tab shows your own assistants on every machine of your cluster as cards: where each runs, whether it is answering Bob right now, what it has attached (browser logins, skills and plugins, MCP servers, secrets), the last time Bob asked it, and a **Bob may ask** switch. There are no ask buttons: just ask Bob. When your message needs what an assistant has, Bob asks it as you, with its own logins and tools, and the line under Bob's reply says so ("Asked Work assistant · Google calendar · 3 s"). The decision model picks which assistants a message needs, in one request for all of them; below your confidence threshold, or without a decision model, Bob's agent picks. An assistant whose project is shared with other people cannot be asked, and its card says why. Webhook-started turns never ask. Click an assistant to open its own chat, where Bob's questions are marked as his. From 1700 px wide, the same list is also a column between the board and Bob's chat that you can fold away.

## Clusters

The **Clusters** tab shows what other people in your clusters are running, grouped by machine, and only through what they share with you: a project shared with a cluster, and its conversations. Your own machines are not listed (they are on the board). Click a card to read that conversation; nothing on this tab can act on another person's agent. Bob can answer questions about them ("what is the team doing?"): when a message is about other people's work, Bob is given a short summary of the shared agents that are running, and the line under the reply says so.

## Telegram

In Bob's settings, **Telegram** connects Bob's own bot: paste the token from @BotFather and Joint Bob sets it up, then open the one-time link (it works once and expires after 10 minutes) to link your own Telegram account. The bot answers only that account, in a private chat: every other sender gets no reply, and a group it is added to is left at once. Whatever you can do in Bob's chat you can do from Telegram, including allowing or blocking a Guardian review, so whoever controls the linked Telegram account controls Bob; turn on Telegram's two-step verification. Bob sends you what matters (steps waiting for your OK, urgent items, reports, things that need you) with buttons, unless monitoring is paused. The machine needs an address Telegram can reach, such as a relay.

## What was removed with the canvas

The canvas panes, pages and split layouts, the conversation shortcuts you could give a pane, the **Add to canvas** actions, and the saved canvas layout are gone. Other keyboard shortcuts stay, in Settings → Shortcuts; the pane and page commands are no longer listed. A saved canvas view opens the control center. Older machines in a cluster that still send canvas shortcuts are ignored.

## On your other machines

Your chat with Bob is yours, not the machine's: it follows you to each of your machines (twins), and to a machine you are signed in on as a visitor, so you can stop on the Mac and go on from the homeserver or your phone. Nobody else's machine gets it.

- **What follows you:** every message, yours and Bob's, with its labels and buttons, in the order it was said; that you use Bob, with your Bob settings (pausing Bob on one machine pauses him on all); Bob's memory topics; and what Bob's review checks found, so every machine's board shows it. When the chat first starts to follow you, each machine sends its last 500 messages once.
- **Answers continue.** Bob on the machine you are using reads the whole chat when he decides what you mean, so "that one" still means the conversation you talked about on the other machine.
- **Buttons work anywhere.** A button acts on something that lives on the machine that wrote the message (a Guardian review, a scheduled report, a running conversation). Pressed on another machine, the press is carried to that one and run there, as you; the answer shows at once, and the buttons go away on every machine. Only your own machines can carry a press. If that machine is off, Bob says so; press again when it is back.

## Limits

- Bob's reports, incoming triggers and the Telegram link stay on the machine where they were set up (their messages reach all your machines, see **On your other machines**). A Telegram bot is answered from one machine only, because two machines reading the same bot take each other's messages; it shows what Bob says on every machine.
- Bob answers for an agent through auto respond (with Bob's own agent and stop instructions), or tells a stopped agent to go on after a review check. When auto respond stops, Bob brings the conversation to you with buttons and the review check stays out of that turn; Bob does not turn auto respond on again by himself, because every stop reason is one where you decide.
- Bob's own actions (moving jobs, stopping turns, changing or deleting automations) do not go to Guardian: each one waits for your button in the chat, whatever Guardian would have said. Pausing or resuming an automation you named happens at once.
- Webhooks are created from the Automations tab, so their secret address is never in the chat.
- Bob's Telegram bot reads text only. A voice message gets a request to type it, until the assistants' speech to text is released.
